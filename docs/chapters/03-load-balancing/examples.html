Load balancing in production

⚠️ Not yet source-checked. These case studies summarize public engineering posts and talks, and some details may be simplified or wrong. Before you repeat a specific number or design in an interview, check it against the company's own write-up. Found an error, or a source that confirms a section? Add it to the tracking issue. Sourced fixes are the most welcome contribution here.

Google · Maglev

Google's 2016 NSDI paper describes Maglev as a software network load balancer. Routers distribute packets to Maglev instances using equal-cost multipath routing. Each instance selects service endpoints and uses consistent hashing and connection tracking to reduce disruption to existing connections during failures. The paper reports that a single Maglev machine could saturate a 10 Gbps link using small packets in the setup it evaluated. That is a historical packet-processing result for Maglev. Google's 2016 Maglev paper

Apply it to Trail Notes. When a backend disappears, distinguish what should happen to new requests from what can happen to a connection already assigned to the failed backend.

Netflix · Routing and overload

Netflix's original Zuul write-up describes using Ribbon for outbound client-side load balancing. Its November 2020 engineering post describes how Zuul shed lower-priority requests progressively under pressure to protect playback. The team reported that its streaming availability metric held steady during the incident shown in the post. Netflix's original Zuul write-up · Netflix's 2020 load-shedding write-up

Apply it to Trail Notes. In the hypothetical lesson workload, protect the guide request first during a sustained capacity shortfall. Optional work, such as recommendations, may be a candidate for shedding. Decide which requests qualify and measure whether essential requests still meet their latency target.
